# Personal data protection

The customer is a data subject when the information concerns an identified or identifiable natural person. A company is not itself a natural-person data subject, but information about representatives, owners, guarantors, employees, and contacts can be personal data. Depending on actual purposes and processing, the institution may be the database owner/responsible party and providers may act as processors; Legal must map roles rather than assume them from a system boundary.

Identify purpose and legal basis by data category: identity, income, history, guarantors, representatives, and risk profile. Consent to a bureau or identity query is not generic consent for every later use and does not replace another lawful basis. Retain the notice/version/date/channel and manage rights, objection, or revocation as applicable. A right request must be routed to the institution's designated process; the application module must preserve evidence of request, scope, response, and any lawful restriction.

Implement minimization, accuracy, need-based access, query traceability, retention/blocking, and data-subject rights handling. Credit scoring is profiling when it uses automated personal-data processing to evaluate a natural person's circumstances; document purpose, categories, data quality, effects, and a meaningful human review route. Avoid unnecessary sensitive data. Assess international transfers and vendors before use. Under DS 016-2024-JUS art. 34, specified high-impact personal-data incidents require notice to ANPD within 48 hours of awareness; qualifying effects on the data subject can trigger communication to that person, and digital incidents may also involve the National Digital Security Center. This is conditional, not a blanket 48-hour rule for every incident. The processor must promptly inform the responsible party under art. 36. Legal/Privacy must confirm triggers, content, and current ANPD guidance for each event.

Law 29733 and DS 016-2024-JUS have been effective since 31 Mar 2025. The responsible party registers applicable personal-data databases; this is an institutional obligation, not a claim that a software module itself is registered. LendCore should support evidence and export for rights requests, authorized blocking/correction, incident records, and retention decisions. This document is neither an impact assessment nor legal opinion. See C-09 and official links in `regulatory-sources.md`.
